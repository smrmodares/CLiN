Research question

How reliable are LLMs as judges of creativity?

Across zero-shot, few-shot, reference-based, ensemble, and multi-agent settings, LLM judgments remain prompt-sensitive and are weakest on highly subjective dimensions. This motivates an alternative for the more structured TTCT-derived dimensions.

Findings

Simple proxies remain competitive with LLM judges

The experiments separate two questions: how well LLMs judge creativity, and whether transparent objective proxies can recover structured human judgments.

CLiN vs. strongest zero-shot judge

Comparable on originality & fluency; significantly better on elaboration

On human-authored texts, the paired item-level bootstrap comparison against Claude 3.7 Sonnet finds no significant difference for originality or fluency, while CLiN significantly outperforms it on elaboration.

Abstract

About the paper

Evaluating creativity in large language model outputs remains challenging because creativity is inherently subjective. We examine the reliability of LLMs as evaluators of short Persian literary texts across multiple evaluation settings and prompt designs. LLM judgments show limited alignment on highly subjective dimensions and substantial sensitivity to prompt formulation. Motivated by these findings, we investigate whether objective and interpretable proxy metrics can provide a reliable alternative for structured TTCT-derived dimensions. CLiN evaluates originality through novelty, fluency through lexical idea diversity, and elaboration through n-gram diversity. These simple proxies achieve human alignment comparable to or better than the strongest LLM judge in the studied setting while substantially reducing evaluation cost.
